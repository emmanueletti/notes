# Brand and positioning, 2026-11-21

Direction for the Workbench 2.0 marketing site, supplies and content. The founders decide; this captures the direction and advice.

## Position

Workbench feels like a cool, casual private bank, not a SaaS product. Classy, calm, personal, quietly excellent.

It champions ethical and sustainable jewellery: repair, custom work, and the independent shops who care how things are made. The honest centre is repair, because a repaired piece stays in use instead of being replaced. Every ethical claim must be backed by something real.

## Public line and long-term vision

Decided 2026-11-21:

- **Public, now**: "the back office for independent jewellers that runs itself". Only what is true today or launching soon (photo intake, speed, the Shopify fit).
- **Long term**: the Shopify for independent jewellery brands. Everything a brand needs to run and grow, and most of it runs itself. Workbench wins when its shops make money, so growth tools for shops (getting found by AI and search, review asks, care reminders, quote follow-up, paid growth add-ons) are core, not out of scope.
- **When the public line widens**: once at least one growth tool is live and working for real shops, move the headline toward the long-term line. "Runs itself" stays as how Workbench does it.
- Boutique shops still want their custom commission conversations kept personal; growth tools help the jeweller reach clients, they don't talk for them.

## The back office that runs itself

The core position: Workbench is the back office for independent jewellers that runs itself. Hands-free, not loud. Ramp beat Concur and Expensify by removing work, not adding features; Workbench does the same for repair and custom work.

It fits the private bank: a private bank quietly handles the paperwork while the client gets on with life. Workbench handles the admin so the jeweller stays at the bench and with their clients.

Working line:

> Workbench is the back office for independent jewellers that runs itself. Jobs come in from a photo, an email or a word, everything is instant, and it works with the Shopify or Square you already use, so your time goes to the craft and your clients.

For wholesale, the same promise as volume: "1,000 jobs a month without retyping a single store form."

### Against the market

| | What it is | Wins on | Weak spot for young operators |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gem Logic, The Edge, Jewel360 | The whole store in one system | Breadth: point of sale, inventory, accounting | Heavy, priced per user and store, replaces Square and Shopify |
| RepairDesk, RepairShopr | Repair software for phone and computer shops | Repair counters in general | Not built for jewellery or custom work |
| FixyFlow, QuantumRepair | Light repair trackers | Price | Everything typed by hand, generic |
| Workbench | A back office that does the work for you | Speed and automation | |

Gem Logic and The Edge want to replace Square and Shopify, so they can't be their best partner. Workbench can.

### Proof points

Each must be true before we say it.

1. Never type: photo intake, forwarded emails, voice (`2026-11-21-machine-automation-strategy.md`). Not built yet.
2. Instant: the speed work in 2.0.
3. Works with what you have: Shopify first, then Square for SarahBijoux.
4. Made with taste: design, supplies, the magazine.
5. A person behind it: the founder as named contact, setup in person.

Rule: lead with speed, taste and the Shopify fit until photo intake is proven at Jewellery Clinic Wholesale. Then launch "never type" as the headline, with Willy's before-and-after numbers and the filmmaker's video.

## What a private bank does that SaaS doesn't

| Private bank | Workbench |
| --- | --- |
| Calm, few words, nothing flashy | Short copy. No "supercharge", no rocket emoji, no feature walls. |
| A named person to talk to | The founder as named contact. No chatbot, no ticket queue. |
| You were introduced, not sold to | Referrals, the magazine and stories over ads |
| Quality in the details | Typography, editorial photography of real benches, stationery-grade supplies, how fast and how well we reply |
| Discreet | Shops' brands come first on everything their customers see; Workbench is a small mark |

## Who it should attract

Young independent brands with taste, like Frank Darling and Myler (check what each stands for before referencing them). From general knowledge, brands like Frank Darling present themselves editorially and talk openly about how their pieces are made. They respond to:

- Taste: does it look like something they'd make?
- Being seen next to peers they respect.
- Craft taken seriously.

They don't respond to feature lists or SaaS comparison tables. Comparison pages still serve search, but they're not the voice of the brand.

## How it shows up

- **Marketing site**: editorial photography of real benches and hands, no stock illustrations, excellent typography, short copy, the founder's name and face.
- **Product**: everything feels instant (see the speed decision in `sarahbijoux.md`). Speed is part of the luxury.
- **Supplies**: uncoated paper, one ink colour, the shop's stamp first, biodegradable where it holds up (see `2026-11-21-supply-store-strategy.md`).
- **Replies**: from a person, fast, in the customer's language.

## Content: a publication, not a social account

The founders dislike social media, so Workbench doesn't run as an account that must post.

- **A jewellers' magazine**: one feature a month on an innovative young independent brand with an ethical and sustainable focus, filmed or photographed with the Montreal filmmaker (trade of services). Features chosen on merit, never only customers.
- **Featured brands do the posting**: they share their own feature with their audience. That's the user-generated content.
- **Reshare what shops post**: benches, envelopes, finished repairs.
- **Customer stories, 2027**: video case studies and interviews (see `2026-11-21-plg-strategy.md`).
- **The founder posts only when there's something real**, at most once a week. No daily posting to keep up.

## Watch for

- Ethical claims without substance read as greenwashing and lose exactly the brands we want. Say only what's true and specific.
- "Classy" must never mean slow or hard to reach. Book a demo, the setup call and the price stay obvious.
- Wholesale shops care about speed and volume more than taste. Their pages speak to time saved, in the same calm voice.
