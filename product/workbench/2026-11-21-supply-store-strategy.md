# Supply store strategy, 2026-11-21

Advice for a Workbench supply store. The founders decide the way forward; this captures every idea with a recommendation.

## Decided

- We sell ourselves, starting with a very small stock.
- Supplies are priced at full cost: every cost covered, no profit. Workbench software is where we earn.
- Design is a selling point. Young brands should be proud to hand our supplies to their customers, while the items stay standard enough for any counter.
- Origin: one shop asked for job bags and envelopes. One shop is a signal, not proof.

## Verdict

Sell only things that connect to something done in Workbench (taking in a job, finding it, updating it, handing it to another shop) and that shops reorder. That is the Shopify lesson: its card readers make Shopify the way a store takes payment, they are not a side business. Generic supplies make us a supplies catalogue with no advantage over Stuller or Rio Grande.

Supplies are part of the hands-free position (`2026-11-21-brand-positioning.md`): scan instead of type.

The link is the QR code. Workbench already prints QR codes on the technician copy and the customer stub. Supplies with a unique QR code let a shop scan to start an intake, open a job or change its status. That makes the counter faster, makes switching away costly (a drawer of printed envelopes), and puts our brand on things that travel between trade shops and stores.

## How to judge an item

1. Connects to a Workbench action.
2. Reordered (consumables beat one-off hardware).
3. Design shows to the shop's own customers or to other jewellers.
4. Cheap to stock in small runs.
5. Low support load (hardware brings returns, warranties and "it won't connect").

## Catalogue

### Launch

| Item | Why | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| QR label rolls | Works on any bag, box or tray a shop already has. The cheapest test of whether shops scan at all. | First item. If nobody scans, the rest is stationery. |

| Item | Why | Recommendation |

### Captured, not recommended

| Item | Why not |
| --- | --- |
| Receipt printer | Workbench prints full pages and labels. A third printer adds support with no new action. |
| Job trays and bench organisers | Commodity, bulky to ship, no link to Workbench |
| Polishing cloths for customers | Nice gift, but no link to Workbench |
| Pre-printed job numbers | Clash with each shop's own numbering in Workbench. Use QR codes linked on scan. |

### Never

Tools, solder, polishing supplies, findings, stones and general packaging. That is a supplier catalogue with no tie to Workbench, and it is Stuller's and the family parts business's territory.

## Envelope design brief

- Standard parts: client name, phone, date in, date promised, item, work to be done, estimate and deposit, metal and stones, condition notes, and a tear-off stub with the matching number.
- Size: measure what SarahBijoux and Jewellery Clinic use and match their trays and drawers.
- The shop's brand, not ours: a clear stamp or logo space at the top, Workbench as a small mark on the back flap.
- QR code designed in, not stuck on: a neat corner square with the job number beside it in a matching typeface. Each envelope has its own code, linked to a job when scanned.
- Paper: uncoated off-white or kraft, one ink colour, room to write, thin rules instead of boxes, small capitals for labels. A good stationery shop, not an office catalogue.
- Closure: peel-and-seal for busy wholesale counters, string and button for the premium feel. Possibly two versions.
- Next step: 3 directions side by side (clean stationery, warm kraft, bold modern), front and back, then refine one section at a time.

## Pricing at full cost

Most at-cost pricing loses money because only the item's price is counted. The price covers:

- Buying or printing the item, plus any setup fee spread over the run
- Packaging
- Shipping, charged as its own line at real cost
- Card fees, about 3% of the order
- Our time to pack and ship
- 5 to 10% for misprints, damage and replacements
- Currency conversion on orders outside Canada

Rules:

- Minimum order sizes (for example packs of 250 envelopes), so a small order doesn't cost more to fill than it brings in.
- Review prices against real costs every quarter.

## Running it ourselves

- **Selling**: a Shopify store, which also teaches us Shopify before we build the Shopify app. Or Stripe payment links for the first few orders.
- **Sales tax**: once we sell goods, we're the seller. In Canada, registering for GST/HST and QST becomes required above $30,000 in sales over four quarters (the small supplier threshold), and registering earlier is allowed. Confirm with an accountant before the first sale.
- **Stock**: the smallest run each supplier allows. Digital printing needs no plates, so 250 to 500 envelopes is a reasonable first run with a local Montreal printer.
- **Cheapest first test**: premium blank envelopes plus QR labels and a field layout printed on our own label printer. Feedback without a print run.
- **Returns**: replace misprints and damage. No returns on custom stamps.
- **Hardware support**: one supported model per device, with a short setup guide in the help centre.

## Testing

1. Ask 5 shops what they use today for job bags, envelopes and labels, where they buy them, what they pay and how often they reorder.
2. Give the first QR labels and envelopes to SarahBijoux and one wholesale shop, in exchange for honest feedback.
3. Watch two numbers: do they reorder, and do they scan? Reordering without scanning means a stationery side line, not a software advantage.
4. Only if both hold, add a "Supplies" page inside Workbench.

Metric: shops with QR supplies compared with shops without, on weeks active and jobs scanned. Supplies earn nothing, so they're judged by what they do for the software.
