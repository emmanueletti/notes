must come from savings, which means about **$285k/yr saved**, or $325k/yr net income at a $40k burn.

### Two facts worth keeping visible

**Runway is 75 months, not months.** $250k over $40k/yr. Any decision framed as "I need income
urgently" is miscalibrated. Caveat: only liquid investable assets count. House equity and locked
registered accounts do not.

**Savings rate is the real weapon.** At $40k burn the savings rate is roughly 88% of net. Most
people chasing this number have to earn double to save the same amount. The frugality is worth more
than a promotion.

**$2M is a 2% withdrawal rate at this burn.** Independence costs closer to $1.25M. $2M is a
deliberate overshoot, chosen knowingly, not an error. Noting it so the choice stays a choice.

## Paths, ranked

### 1. Workbench to $1-1.5M ARR

5-7 years. Owned. Doing the intended thing. Ceiling around $3-4M ARR, which is $1M+/yr owner earnings
or a $12-25M exit. Highest ceiling, highest variance, slowest floor.

Two constraints from the market research: Canada alone tops out near $200k ARR, so the US is most of
the plan rather than a later phase. And blended ACV matters more than customer count, so the move off
$40/mo is the single highest-leverage change available.

### 2. FAANG, US comp, no state tax

The fallback, and a good one.

- **TN visa.** The Canadian advantage nobody else has. No lottery, granted at the border often same
  day, 3 years renewable, ~$50. Category is Computer Systems Analyst. Requires a bachelor's, or a
  post-secondary diploma plus 3 years experience. **Verify this credential before planning around
  it.** Tied to one employer, no immigrant intent.
- **Target Seattle.** Washington has no state income tax, worth $50-65k/yr against California at
  this income. Amazon, Microsoft, Meta, Google all have large orgs. 2.5 hours from Vancouver.
- **Math:** Senior at $450k TC nets ~$305k. At a $55k Seattle burn, save $250k/yr, reach $1.8M in 5
  years. Promote to staff at ~$650k and it is $2M in 4.3 years.

Employer notes:

- Netflix: all cash $500-700k, no vesting handcuffs, keeper test means no floor. For a 4-year cash
  run rather than a career, the downside is smaller than it looks.
- Meta: front-loaded RSUs, measurable-impact culture, explicit low-performer targets.
- Google: slowest promo, most process, gentlest.
- Amazon: 5/15/40/40 vest means year 3 comp drops when sign-on expires. Model before comparing
  offers.

Gap to senior is presentational, not real. Gap to staff is cross-team influence, which is where prep
time should go since the level is worth ~$200k/yr in this plan.

**Before signing anything, get a written IP carve-out for Workbench.** Easiest at offer stage,
impossible after.

### 3. Consulting at Shopify-ecosystem rates

$250-350/hr. Covers the $40k burn in roughly 150 billable hours a year. This is the unlock: at this
burn, cash neutrality is a light retainer, not a job. Leaves the $250k compounding untouched and
buys most of the year for product work.

### 4. Quant dev

Investigated and rejected for this goal.

- The researcher and trader roles are effectively closed at 32 without the pedigree. The dev roles
  are open.
- Bar is harder than FAANG: C++ depth, cache and kernel level systems, plus a pedigree prior that
  Rails does not satisfy.
- 12-18 months of prep before interviewable. Crypto market makers (Wintermute, GSR, Auros) are the
  realistic entry at 6-9 months, then hop.
- Five days in office, NYC or Chicago, and most firms contractually forbid outside work. **It kills
  the product company outright.**
- Dominated by path 2: Netflix pays comparable cash, hires in months not years, and does not
  require learning C++ from scratch. Quant dev only wins at the $700k+ tier, which is 3-5 years
  after entry.

The appeal of quant work is real and worth naming: an unambiguous scoreboard, performance work as
the product rather than a chore, depth that does not expire, the lowest bullshit ratio in the
industry, $1M+ as a pure IC with no management ladder. What it does not offer is ownership,
autonomy, or building something people touch. Those are the four things this whole plan is about.

### 5. Trading own capital

Rejected on arithmetic, not skill. $250k to $2M is 8x. At 30%/yr that is 7.9 years; at 50% it is
5.1. Medallion, the best record in history, does roughly 40% net. Returns that could get there fast
are leveraged with fat left tails, and this capital is simultaneously the runway.

The binding constraint is capital size, not edge. **Trading scales with capital, software scales
with labour.** Play the game the assets fit.

## Sequencing

1. Consulting retainer covering $40k/yr. Roughly 150 billable hours.
2. Everything else on Workbench, aimed at US expansion and higher ACV.
3. Do not spend the $250k. It compounds untouched.
4. Keep FAANG readiness warm at ~4 hrs/week. Insurance, not a plan.
5. Decision date: Q1 2027. Needs a real customer trajectory or path 2 activates.

Floor is $2M at 37 via salary. Ceiling is $2M at 35 via the business. Both stay live at once.

## Cross-border items, before any US move

- **Departure tax.** Leaving Canada triggers deemed disposition on non-registered investments.
  Unrealized gains crystallize on exit. Model before moving.
- **TFSA.** Not recognized by the IRS. Becomes taxable, likely with PFIC filing problems if it holds
  Canadian ETFs. Usually correct to collapse or restructure before departure.
- **RRSP.** Treaty-recognized, fine to leave, file the election.
- Get a cross-border accountant once. Four figures, saves five.

## The thesis this all serves

Left Shopify to apply elite maker training to tools for independents and underdogs. That is the
actual objective; $2M is the constraint that makes it survivable, not the goal.

Three things learned that support it rather than undermine it:

**Underdog does not mean broke.** The version that fails serves hobbyists and tiny nonprofits with
no budget. The version that works serves operators who are outgunned but profitable: a solo lawyer
billing $300/hr, an HVAC contractor netting $200k, a jeweller marking up 3x. Clio, Jobber, Wave,
Faire and Shopify are all Canadian and all built on exactly this. You are not subsidizing the poor,
you are arming the outnumbered.

**Coding agents made this easier, not harder.** The reason nobody serves small operators profitably
was never build cost, it was cost to serve: onboarding, migration, support, per-customer edge cases.
Agents collapse exactly that. The market that was structurally unservable is becoming servable and
almost nobody has repriced around it.

**Venture money kills the mission on a four-year clock.** Every company serving small customers gets
pulled upmarket because one $5k/mo customer is easier than fifty $100/mo customers. Mailchimp did
it, ServiceTitan did it. Staying down-market is a permanent, payable choice, and bootstrapping is
the mechanism that protects it. 37signals and Fathom are the proof.
