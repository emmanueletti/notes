# Operating Model

## Growth philosophy

We grow by **multiplication, not addition.**

Every person we hire is leveraged by AI to operate at the scale of a small team. We will likely never exceed 15 people. We will ship at the quality and ambition of companies ten times our size.

This is not "stay tiny forever." It is **deliberate undergrowth.** Every hire is a 5x multiplier, not a 1x addition. WhatsApp at 55 people serving 900M users was the pre-AI proof. AI makes that model accessible to companies that are not sitting on viral network effects.

## The AI multiplier — and where it does not apply

The multiplier is uneven. It is high for execution work, near-zero for judgment work.

- **AI 2-3x's:** code, design iteration, copy, support, ops automation, research synthesis, content drafting.
- **AI does not help with:** judgment, alignment, taste calls, hiring decisions, founder-level strategic choices, relationships with users.

As we grow, the bottleneck migrates. At 2 people, the constraint is throughput. At 12, throughput is solved, and the constraint becomes **decision quality, alignment, and taste at the seams between people.** AI does not help with that — if anything it makes it worse, because the team ships faster than judgment can keep up.

The failure mode to watch for: a small AI-leveraged team that out-builds its own ability to decide what should be built. The output looks impressive and the product gets worse.

## Hiring philosophy

When each person has 5x leverage, a misaligned senior hire does 5x the damage.

- **Hiring bar = co-founder bar** for the first 8-10 hires. Not employee bar.
- **Hire for judgment, not skills.** Skills are now multiplied by AI; judgment is not.
- **Hire slowly.** A wrong hire at this scale costs us a year. The cost of waiting is much smaller than the cost of being wrong.
- **Pay top-of-market.** We can — we are hiring 5 people, not 50. Generous comp attracts top talent who would otherwise be at FAANG.
- **Each hire owns a domain.** Not a slice. Whole product, whole function. Ownership is what motivates the people we want.
- **Default to never hiring.** Make ourselves prove the case for each role. The default is "we should be able to do this with AI" until it is genuinely not true.

## Team rhythm at each scale

**At 2:** vibes-based. Constant async + a weekly sync. Everything is shared context.

**At 5:** culture starts to form whether we plan it or not. Write down the principles before they crystallize wrong. Every hire from here is hired *into* a culture, not building it with us.

**At 8-12:** real structure required. Owners per area. Clear decision rights. Written-down processes for the load-bearing rituals (weekly review, hiring, launch).

**At 15:** we should be deeply suspicious of any further growth. Default answer: no.

## What we will not do

- We will not hire to fill seats.
- We will not hire because we are tired (we will rest instead, or fix the upstream cause).
- We will not hire generalists at scale (one or two early, then specialists with deep range).
- We will not outsource the things that define us — voice, taste, user relationships, hiring.
- We will not let coordination overhead grow faster than output. If it is, we are too big.

## The compounding asset

The team is the product behind the products.

A small team that has worked together for years, shares a north star, has internalized the principles, and operates with high trust will outperform any larger team in our weight class — and can take on companies in heavier weight classes.

That asset takes 3-5 years to build. We start now. We protect it from anything that would dilute it.
