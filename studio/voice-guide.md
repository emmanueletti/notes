# Voice Guide

The reference for any written voice in the studio's name. Used by the founders, the `scribe` skill, and any future contributor.

Three voices live here:

1. **Founder voice** — first-person singular, used on Twitter, the long-form essays, and any public-facing founder communication.
2. **Workbench voice** — used on Instagram, the website, in-product copy, and customer communication for Workbench specifically.
3. **Studio voice** — first-person plural, used for ITS US STUDIO communications (about us, careers, future product launches). Less used today; will matter more as the portfolio grows.

For all three: the underlying rules are the same. Honesty, specificity, no AI tells. The differences are in subject, register, and audience.

Last updated: 2026-04-18

---

## Universal rules (apply to all voices)

### Always

- **Lead with the thing that matters.** No throat-clearing. Earn the reader's time in sentence one.
- **Specifics over abstractions.** Names, numbers, examples. Not adjectives.
- **Short by default.** Not all sentences. Vary rhythm. But default short.
- **Honest.** Including about what we do not know, what is not working, what we got wrong.
- **One idea per post.** Many posts can be one idea each. One post should not be three ideas glued together.
- **The reader's time is not free.** Every sentence earns its place or gets cut.

### Never

- **Em-dash as a comma.** AI tell. Use commas, periods, or parentheses.
- **The "it's not just X, it's Y" cadence.** AI tell. Even when true, find another way to say it.
- **Tricolons on every line.** "We build, we ship, we learn." AI tell. Vary structure.
- **"Delve," "dive in," "in conclusion," "let's explore," "in today's fast-paced world."** AI tells. Cut on sight.
- **"Industry-leading," "best-in-class," "game-changer," "10x," "next-generation."** Marketing-speak. Not us.
- **"Here's why," "the secret to," "what nobody tells you about."** Engagement-bait openings. Skip.
- **Emoji used as decoration.** Use emoji sparingly, only when it adds meaning. Never strings of them.
- **Hashtag walls.** One or two only when functionally needed (Instagram occasionally).
- **Generic compliments.** "Great post!" "Love this!" Either be specific or do not engage.
- **Manufactured urgency.** "Don't miss this." "Last chance." Not us.

### When in doubt

Cut. The post is almost always better shorter.

## Founder voice (Twitter, long-form, public communication)

### Who speaks

The founder, by name. First-person singular ("I"). Sometimes "we" when speaking for both founders or the studio's collective view, but the default is "I."

### Who listens

- Other indie founders, especially the taste-driven and craft-focused
- Designers, design-engineers, and product people
- The longer-tail audience for our future products
- Trade journalists and indie-tech writers
- Other practitioners we respect

### Tone (5 adjectives)

Honest. Specific. Opinionated. Dry. Calm.

### What this voice is

- A working founder thinking out loud
- Specific about the day's work, this week's lesson, this month's number
- Willing to disagree with prevailing wisdom when there is reason
- Comfortable with what is unresolved or unknown
- Quietly confident, not performative
- Sometimes wrong, willing to say so

### What this voice is not

- Influencer-coded
- Self-help or motivational
- Lecture-coded ("you should…")
- Hype
- Performative humility ("just a small indie")
- Contrarian for its own sake

### Sentence patterns

- Specific noun, specific verb. "Visited four jewelry shops in Toronto today" beats "Spent some time in the field today."
- Number-driven openings work. "10 customers. Here's what they all have in common."
- Setup-and-turn structure. State a thing, then complicate it. ("Most founders…. We are doing the opposite, and here is why.")
- Question-and-answer in one post. ("How long until in-person stops scaling? About 200 customers.")

### Topics in scope

- The build, with specifics (visits, conversations, decisions, prices)
- The thesis (portfolio bet, AI's real limits, FIRE-funded patient indie)
- The vertical (jewelry/watch repair, what surprises us about it)
- Disagreement with indie-orthodoxy where warranted
- Numbers in public (revenue, customer count, conversion, price tests)
- Process artifacts (decision logs, the milestone framework, the in-person playbook)
- Books, ideas, work outside the domain that influenced us

### Topics out of scope

- Politics (unless directly relevant to indie business — rare)
- Hot takes on companies we do not work with
- Personal life beyond what is necessary for the studio story
- Other founders' failures (do not punch down)
- Cryptocurrency, NFTs, web3 (not our domain)

### Example posts (good)

> Visited four jewelry shops in Toronto today. The pattern across all of them: every owner can name the exact moment in the workflow where they lose money — and none of them have a tool that fixes it.
>
> That's the gap.

> Raised our price from $40 to $60 CAD/mo today for new Workbench customers. Existing 10 grandfathered.
>
> The math forced it. In-person acquisition costs us 3-5 founder hours per customer. At $40, the unit economics were broken.
>
> Will share what conversion does at 60 days.

> Most "AI multiplies your headcount" posts skip the part where in-person sales is the one function AI cannot multiply.
>
> If your buyer doesn't live online, your team doesn't either. We learned this the hard way for Workbench. Two founders walking into jewelry shops, no shortcut.

### Example posts (bad — do not ship)

> ❌ "🚀 Just hit 10 paying customers — feeling so grateful for this journey 🙏 What a ride!"
>   *(Humblebrag, emojis as decoration, no specifics, no useful info.)*

> ❌ "Here's the unpopular truth about indie SaaS that nobody tells you: 1/ You need to grind 2/ Customers won't come to you 3/ Sales is everything"
>   *(Engagement bait opener, threadbait numbering, generic content, would apply to any business.)*

> ❌ "It's not just about building a product — it's about building a movement."
>   *(AI cadence, vague, sounds like every other founder.)*

> ❌ "We're excited to announce that we've been working hard on something special. Stay tuned!"
>   *(Throat-clearing, no actual news, manufactured anticipation.)*

## Workbench voice (Instagram, website, in-product, customer communication)

### Who speaks

Workbench, the product. Sometimes first-person plural for the team behind it ("we"). The product itself is treated as quiet — the trade and the craft are loud.

### Who listens

- Jewelry and watch shop owners and managers
- Bench jewelers, watchmakers, technicians
- Trade insiders — diamond dealers, casting houses, equipment suppliers, sales reps
- Trade press
- Adjacent professionals (designers, appraisers, gemologists)
- The jewelry-curious public on Instagram

### Tone (5 adjectives)

Visual-first. Quiet. Craft-respecting. Specific. Of the trade.

### What this voice is

- A guest in the shop who knows the difference between a halo setting and a bezel
- More interested in the piece than in the software
- Respectful of the time and attention of busy shop owners
- Bilingual-aware (EN/FR for Canadian content)
- Comfortable letting the photo do the work

### What this voice is not

- Loud
- Salesy
- Generic SaaS marketing
- Talking down to the trade ("Did you know your shop could be more efficient?")
- Trying to be witty
- Talking about itself

### Sentence patterns

- Photo carries the post. Caption is brief.
- Name the piece, name the shop (with permission), name the technique.
- Quietly attribute or describe before any product mention.
- Product references are factual, not promotional.

### Topics in scope

- Specific repairs and custom work (with shop permission)
- The craft — techniques, tools, materials
- The shops we work with — spotlights, behind-the-counter moments
- Trade events and shows
- Workflow moments that are visually interesting (intake, completion, ticket walking through shop)
- Bilingual content where appropriate (EN/FR)

### Topics out of scope

- Software feature announcements (unless directly story-relevant)
- Pricing
- Comparisons with competitors
- Founder lifestyle content
- Jokes about jewelry/watches (the trade takes itself seriously; respect it)
- Industry takedowns

### Example posts (good)

> [Photo: a freshly resized engagement ring next to the original size markings]
>
> Half-size up, retipped four prongs, polished. Half a day at @[shop]. Back to its owner before the weekend.

> [Photo: signed intake form and the piece beside it, on a workbench]
>
> Every job at @[shop] starts with a signed intake. Photos. Description. Terms. Twelve seconds at the counter that prevent twelve hours of dispute later.

> [Photo: bench jeweler at work, hands and tools, no faces]
>
> @[shop] does about 40 repairs a week. Every one of them passes through these hands. Workbench keeps track so they don't have to.

### Example posts (bad — do not ship)

> ❌ "🎉 New feature alert! Now you can attach photos to job tickets. Try Workbench free for 30 days! Link in bio. #SaaS #Jewelers #Productivity"
>   *(Salesy, generic, hashtag-stuffed, makes the product the hero.)*

> ❌ "Did you know that 67% of jewelry shops still use spreadsheets? It's time to upgrade."
>   *(Stat-of-questionable-origin opener, condescending to the trade.)*

> ❌ "Workbench is the industry-leading platform for jewelry and watch shops. Streamline your workflow today!"
>   *(Marketing-speak, marketing-speak, marketing-speak.)*

## Studio voice (ITS US STUDIO general communication)

### When used

- The studio's "about" page
- Multi-product communications
- Future product launches
- Collective statements that speak for both founders

### Tone

Same as founder voice, with two differences:

- First-person plural ("we") instead of singular
- Slightly more measured — the studio is more deliberate than any single founder's stream of consciousness

### Sentence patterns

- "We build…" "We refuse…" "We will not…" — declarative, principled
- Pulls language directly from the north star where appropriate
- Avoids the flippancy that occasionally works in founder voice

### Example (good)

> ITS US STUDIO is a two-founder bootstrapped studio building a portfolio of beloved products in niches abandoned by bloated incumbents. We grow by multiplication, not addition. We refuse institutional capital, fear-driven features, and the loud user. We are betting that the next generation of beloved software will not come from venture-scale companies — and we are building like it.

### Example (bad — do not ship)

> ❌ "ITS US STUDIO is a forward-thinking, AI-powered product studio leveraging cutting-edge technology to disrupt traditional verticals."
>   *(Every word is a buzzword. None of it is true to who we are.)*

## How to use this guide with `scribe`

When invoking the `scribe` skill (`~/notes/skills/scribe/`), reference this file:

> "Draft 3 angles for a Twitter post about [topic]. Use founder voice per `~/notes/studio/voice-guide.md`."

> "Draft an Instagram caption for this photo: [photo]. Workbench voice. Shop is @[name]."

The scribe skill is instructed to read this guide before drafting. You should still edit every output before posting.

## How to use this guide with `critic`

When asking critic to review a draft post, reference the voice guide so it can check against specific rules:

> "Review this Twitter draft against `voice-guide.md` founder voice rules."

## When to update this guide

- When you discover a new pattern that works.
- When you ship something you regret — capture the lesson here.
- When you find yourself correcting the same thing in `scribe` outputs more than twice.
- After every monthly social retro, review and revise.

This guide is alive. It should sharpen over time as the voice does.
