# Social Strategy

How the studio shows up online. Two voices, two audiences, one anchor. Built for compounding consistency over viral peaks.

For voice rules and example posts, see `~/notes/studio/voice-guide.md`.

Last updated: 2026-04-18

---

## The two-audience truth

We have two audiences who do not overlap. We must serve both, separately.

**Audience 1: Workbench's buyers** — jewelry and watch shop owners. They live on Instagram if they are online at all, and on the trade-press circuit if they are not. They are *not* on Twitter and they will not find us through founder-build content.

**Audience 2: The studio's audience** — other indie founders, the taste-driven design/tech community, future customers of future products. They live on Twitter, in indie founder communities, and read longform from people they trust.

Collapsing these into one feed fails both. Different stories, different platforms, different voices.

## The frame for the next 90 days

The first 90 days is **not** about growth metrics. It is about establishing voice and cadence. Most indie social efforts die at month 2 because they were optimizing for follower count when they should have been optimizing for "did we publish this week, and was it any good."

The compounding asset is the consistent, distinctive voice. Audience growth follows it.

## The platform stack

### Primary: Founder voice on Twitter / X

- **Who speaks:** One founder, signed name. Not a brand account. Personal voice.
- **Audience served:** The studio's audience. Builds the portfolio brand.
- **Why this:** The single best place to build trust with indie/tech/taste-driven readers. Contrarian takes compound here. Numbers in public compound here. The build journey lives here.
- **Frequency:** 3 substantive posts/week minimum. Plus replies and engagement on others' work.
- **Success metric:** Reply quality, saves, DMs from real people. Not follower count, not likes.

### Primary: Workbench Instagram

- **Who speaks:** Workbench-the-product. Visual-first. Quiet voice in captions.
- **Audience served:** Jewelry/watch shop owners, trade insiders, jewelry-curious general public.
- **Why this:** Jewelry is one of the most visual industries on earth. The trade lives on Instagram. Beautiful repairs and craft moments are native content.
- **Frequency:** 2 posts/week minimum, more when visiting shops.
- **Success metric:** Saves, DMs from shop owners, comments from named people in the trade.

### Anchor: One long-form piece per month

- **Who speaks:** Founder voice. Honest, specific, with numbers.
- **Where it lives:** Founder blog, Substack, or published natively as a Twitter long-form post. Pick one platform and hold it.
- **Why this:** The piece you write monthly is what makes everything else compound. Excerpts feed Twitter. Reframings pitch to trade publications. The body becomes the thing you point new people to.
- **Frequency:** 1 piece per month, non-negotiable.
- **Success metric:** Whether you would proudly send it to someone whose taste you respect.

### Skip for now

- **LinkedIn.** Lifestyle-founder content is brand-corrosive. Revisit only if a specific Workbench-buyer angle emerges (some shop owners are on LinkedIn).
- **TikTok.** Wrong demographic for both audiences.
- **Threads.** No audience yet. Not worth fragmenting attention.
- **YouTube.** Eventually valuable but expensive to start. Wait until there is a real video story to tell.
- **Hacker News.** Useful occasional appearance for studio-thesis essays, not a daily channel.
- **Multiple platforms simultaneously.** The diluted-mediocre-everywhere trap. Master two before adding a third.

## The angles only ITS US STUDIO has

These are unmistakably ours. Most of our content should mine these veins.

1. **FIRE-funded patient indie.** Almost no one in indie SaaS has 10 years of runway because they personally chose financial independence first. Tell that story honestly, without bragging, with the discipline lens.
2. **Two-founder dynamic.** Two-founder stories are common. Specifics about how *these* two work together are not. Lean into what is true.
3. **The Pixar/A24 thesis with conviction.** Most indies pitch one cool thing. We have a 10-year vision. Articulate it. People follow vision more than products.
4. **The honest in-person reality.** While every other founder is talking about AI agents replacing sales, we are walking into jewelry stores in Toronto. That contrast is the post.
5. **What AI can and cannot multiply.** A sharper view than 99% of "AI startup" Twitter holds.
6. **Numbers in the open.** Customer counts, conversion rates, pricing tests, what worked, what didn't. Indie audience eats this. Builds trust faster than anything else.
7. **The vertical.** Jewelry/watch repair is genuinely interesting — high-value items, traditional craftspeople, dramatic stakes. Most readers have never thought about it.

These angles are not in any other indie founder's feed.

## Formats that work

### For Twitter (founder voice)

- **Behind-the-craft posts.** "Spent today in 4 jewelry shops in Toronto. Three things that surprised me…"
- **Decision posts.** "We just raised our price 50%. Here's why and what we expect to happen."
- **Numbers-in-public posts.** Monthly: revenue, customers, what worked, what didn't.
- **Thesis posts.** The bigger ideas, including pushback against indie/AI orthodoxy.
- **Process screenshots.** The decision doc, the milestone framework, the in-person playbook (sanitized).
- **Replies and quotes.** The undervalued half of Twitter. Thoughtful replies on others' posts compound visibility without performing.

### For Workbench Instagram

- **Shop spotlights.** Beautiful photos of partner shops. Two sentences about their craft. Tag them.
- **Repair stories.** Before/after on dramatic repairs (with shop permission). The piece is the hero.
- **Workflow moments.** The intake photo, the signed terms, the print job ticket walking through the shop. Show the rituals of the trade.
- **Behind-the-scenes from visits.** Founder visiting a shop, with the shop's permission. The trade is built on relationships; show ours forming.
- **Product as quiet character.** Workbench appears in screenshots only when the story actually involves it. Never the headline.

### For the long-form anchor

- **Monthly founder letter.** What we did, what we learned, what we are trying next. Honest, specific, with numbers.
- **Trade-publication pitches.** Same essay, reframed for JCK Magazine, National Jeweler, Canadian Jeweller. They need content; we have it.
- **Thesis essays.** Less frequent. The bigger ideas — operating model, AI limits, the portfolio bet.

## Cadence

| Channel | Frequency | Floor (never below) |
|---|---|---|
| Twitter substantive | 3/week | 1/week |
| Twitter replies/engagement | Daily, 15 min | 3 days/week |
| Instagram | 2/week | 1/week |
| Long-form essay | 1/month | 1 every 6 weeks |

The "floor" is the absolute minimum during a hard week. Below the floor = the system is broken; address before resuming.

## The 30-day inventory rule

Before launching any channel publicly, build a 30-day inventory of posts. Reasons:

- You have buffer for bad weeks.
- You can see whether you have enough material to sustain the cadence.
- You discover voice issues by writing 30 in a row, not by writing 2.
- The first 30 are usually mediocre. Better to throw them away privately than ship them.

## What success looks like at 90 days

- 12+ Twitter substantive posts shipped
- 24+ Instagram posts shipped
- 3 long-form pieces published
- A documented founder voice that is not generic indie
- A documented Workbench voice that is unmistakably of the trade
- At least 5 unsolicited replies/DMs from real people in our audiences
- Zero AI-generated posts shipped without human voice editing

## What success looks like at 12 months

- A founder Twitter audience that includes specific named people you respect — fellow indie founders, designers, taste-driven readers
- A Workbench Instagram followed by named people in the trade — not just shop owners but suppliers, casting houses, trade press
- 1+ trade publication article published with our byline
- 1+ podcast appearance on a bench-jeweler or jewelry-trade show
- A body of long-form work (12 essays minimum) that articulates the studio's worldview clearly enough to stand alone

## What we refuse

- **Posting about posting.** No engagement strategy posts, no growth tips, no follower milestones celebrated.
- **AI-generated posts.** The brand is honesty. AI tells will erode it within months. Use AI to edit; never to draft.
- **Humblebragging.** "Just hit 10 customers, feeling so grateful 🙏" is the indie equivalent of corporate-speak.
- **Engagement bait.** "What's the one thing nobody tells you about…" / "Unpopular opinion:" / threadbait openings.
- **Forced humor.** Sincerity outperforms manufactured wit. Be funny only when it is honest.
- **Outsourced voice.** This voice is the brand. It cannot come from anyone else, including AI agents we control.
- **Platform-juggling.** Two well, before any third.
- **Optimizing for likes.** Replies > likes. Saves > shares. DMs > follows. Real audience signals are quieter and more durable.
- **Posting about competitors directly.** Make the industry better; do not punch down.
- **Trying to go viral.** Viral is a side effect, not a strategy.

## How this fits with the rest of the studio

- **Workbench buyers convert in person, not online.** Social media for Workbench is not lead generation. It is trade reputation, partner reach, and the eventual replacement for cold visits when word-of-mouth takes over.
- **Founder Twitter is the portfolio brand investment.** Pays back over years, not months. Funds the next product launch with an audience already paying attention.
- **Long-form anchors compound in two directions** — into social posts and into trade-publication pitches. One piece, multiple uses.
- **Voice is binding.** Anything published in the studio's name must pass the voice guide. See `voice-guide.md`.

## Operating rituals

- **Monday batch.** First hour of Monday: draft the week's substantive posts. Schedule them. Frees the rest of the week to be present in conversations and shop visits.
- **Daily 15 min.** End of day: 15 min of replies, comments, DMs across both platforms. Not posting — engaging.
- **Friday review.** Weekly review (per `day-one-playbook.md`) includes: did we hit the cadence, did we publish anything we are proud of, what is in the pipeline.
- **Monthly retro.** Specifically reviews social: which posts worked, what we learned about voice, what to write more or less of.
