# Thesis

## The bet

> The next generation of beloved software will not come from venture-scale companies. It will come from small teams with strong taste, building for users that incumbents stopped seeing.
>
> AI has collapsed the cost of building. What is scarce now is judgment — knowing what to build, what to leave out, and who to build it for. Feature parity is no longer defensible. Distribution, taste, and trust are.
>
> We build a portfolio of small, profitable, deeply-loved products in niches where the incumbent has gone soft. Each product stands alone. Together they compound into something a single product cannot: a reputation that earns the next user's trust before they have seen the screen.
>
> We are betting that in an age where anyone can build anything, the companies that win will be the ones people choose to root for.

## Why this works

- **Building costs collapsed faster than distribution costs.** A two-person team can now ship at the quality bar of a 2018 Series B. That arbitrage is real and probably has a 3-5 year window before it normalizes.
- **Niche incumbents are unusually vulnerable right now.** They are distracted by their own AI panic, cutting costs, and unable to ship the small things that matter to small audiences.
- **Portfolio brand compounds in a way single products cannot.** Tiny, A24, Pixar, Studio Ghibli, Panic, Basecamp — all proof that "made by ___" can become a wedge in itself. This is underrated.
- **Recurring revenue + small headcount = a business that is profitable at scales VC-backed companies cannot survive at.** We can win battles they cannot fight.

## What will probably kill us if it does

- **Attention dilution.** Two people across three products means none get the obsessive care that makes them great. The portfolio model only works if each product can run mostly on rails between bursts of focused attention. Most cannot.
- **Distribution is the bottleneck, not building.** We will build faster than we can find audiences. Without a distribution thesis as sharp as the product thesis, we will have a graveyard of beautiful unused things.
- **AI commodifies us too.** Whatever we build, someone with the same tools can clone in a month. Our moat has to be brand, community, and the things AI cannot replicate — taste, point of view, relationship with users.
- **The portfolio takes years to compound.** A24 took a decade. Tiny took fifteen years. We need a financial model that survives the slow years before the brand does any work for us.
- **Niche economics can be brutal.** "Small audience that loves it" can mean $200k ARR or $2M ARR. The difference is whether the niche has money and whether our pricing reflects intensity of love, not market comps.

## The honest read

This is not a *product* bet — it is a *company-as-product* bet. We are not really wagering that we can build great software (we probably can). We are wagering that we can build a brand and operating model that lets a small team sustain craft across multiple products for a decade without burning out, selling out, or fragmenting attention.

That is a harder, more interesting bet. It has been won before. It has been lost more often.

The teams that win it tend to share three things:

1. **Ruthless focus** on one product until it is self-sustaining before starting the next.
2. **A public voice** that builds audience independent of any single product.
3. **Financial discipline** that treats profitability as freedom, not a milestone.

If we build that way, the bet is very live.

## Why now is the right window

- The AI productivity arbitrage is real and time-limited. In 3-5 years it normalizes.
- Incumbents are mid-disruption, looking inward, slow to defend niches.
- Audiences are increasingly skeptical of generic, machine-stamped software and hungry for things made with care.
- Distribution channels (Twitter, niche communities, indie media) still reward authentic voice over budget.

The window is open. It will not stay open forever.
