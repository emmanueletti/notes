# North Star

The always-read document. Re-read at the start of every quarter, before every major decision, and any time the work starts to feel unclear.

---

## What we are

We build small, beloved products for people who have been failed by software that got too big to care.

We are two people today. We will grow deliberately — by multiplication, not addition. Every person we add will be leveraged by AI to operate at the scale of a small team. We will likely never exceed 15 people. We will ship at the quality and ambition of companies ten times our size.

Our advantage is not scale. It is taste, speed, and the ability to give a damn about details no public company can justify.

## Who we serve

People in overlooked corners of established categories. The ones whose workflows the incumbent flattened into a generic feature. The ones who quietly resent the tool they use every day but cannot switch because nothing better exists.

We do not chase TAM. We chase devotion.

## What we are building toward

A portfolio of products that share one trait: **users would be genuinely upset if we shut it down.**

Not "satisfied." Not "engaged." Upset. That is the bar.

If we build five products that clear that bar over the next decade, we have won. If we build fifty that do not, we have failed — even if they make money.

Pixar did not win by making more movies. A24 did not win by outspending Disney. They won by being unmistakably themselves, over and over, until that became the moat. That is the game.

## How we decide

When choosing what to build, what to ship, what to cut, we ask:

1. **Would we use this every day?** If not, we do not ship it.
2. **Does this make the product more itself, or more like everyone else?** Drift toward sameness is the enemy.
3. **Are we adding because users need it, or because we are afraid to say no?** Fear-driven features are how we become the thing we are replacing.
4. **Is this something only we would build?** If a competent team at the incumbent could ship the same thing, we are not far enough out on the edge.

## What we refuse

- We will not raise institutional capital that forces us to grow faster than we can stay good.
- We will not add a feature to win a deal we should not have been in.
- We will not let the roadmap be written by the loudest user.
- We will not ship what we would not show a friend.
- We will not measure ourselves by what is easy to measure.
- We will not hire to fill seats. Every hire is a co-founder-level decision.
- We will not start product #2 until product #1 is profitable and mostly self-running.
- We will not build in stealth. Distribution starts day one.

## What success looks like

Users who tell their friends without being asked. A product that feels handmade in a market of machine-stamped software. A small business that funds the lives of the people who built it, on terms we set. A reputation, accumulated across products, that earns trust before the first screen loads.

Profitability is freedom, not a milestone. We measure success in years, not quarters.

## The meta-principle

Most indie companies fail not from a bad idea but from inconsistency. The setup matters less than holding it. We pick a rhythm we can sustain when we are tired, sick, or discouraged — because we will be all three, often.

Boring discipline. Distinctive output. That is the formula.
