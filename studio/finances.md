## Why the burn is this low

Both founders are FIRE practitioners (Financial Independence / Retire Early). Spending has been intentionally optimized for years before this point. The studio is funded by financial independence, not the other way around.

This is not a temporary "tighten the belt during launch" posture. It is the operating cost of life that the founders have already chosen to live.

## What this means strategically

**This is patient capital.** It is rare. Most indie founders operate on 12-18 months of runway and must reach revenue or die. We have ~10 years.

The strategic implications:

- **We can build correctly, not desperately.** We do not need to compromise the product, the niche, the pricing, or the in-person depth in order to chase short-term revenue.
- **We can wait out the slow channels.** Word-of-mouth, trade reputation, brand-as-distribution — the things that actually compound — all take 18-36 months to start working. We can afford to invest in them.
- **We can refuse bad-fit customers.** Without revenue pressure, we can hold the line on who we serve and what we will not build.
- **We can hold pricing discipline.** No need to discount in panic. We can price for value and walk away from buyers who do not see it.

## What this also means — the trap

**Long runway is the most common cause of indie company drift.**

Without external pressure (running out of money, missing payroll, losing investor patience), the only forcing function is the one we impose on ourselves. Most founders in this position do not impose enough.

Failure mode: month 18 arrives, $250K spent, 30 customers acquired, no compelling reason for it to have taken that long. The story we will tell ourselves at that point is "we were being patient." The truth will be that we did not create artificial milestones with internal consequences.

To prevent this, we maintain an explicit milestone framework — see below.

## The milestone framework

Each cycle and each strategic arc has a target. Each target has an internal consequence if missed. The consequence is not "we run out of money." It is "we admit something is wrong and change course."

### Workbench arc milestones

| Date | Milestone | If missed, the question we owe each other |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-07-15 (Day 90) | 25-35 paying customers, in-person playbook documented, first case study live, pricing test running | "Is the in-person channel actually working at this rate? If not, why?" |
| 2027-01-15 (Month 9) | 75 paying customers, Toronto + Montreal saturated, trade publication presence, first inbound from referral | "Is the path-to-200 plausible at this trajectory? If not, what changes — channel mix, market, product?" |
| 2027-10-15 (Month 18) | 150-200 paying customers, >30% inbound mix, trade reputation real | "Is Workbench worth another 18-month bet, or is the right move to launch product #2 now?" |

### What the consequence looks like

If we miss a milestone, we do not pretend nothing happened. We:

1. Run a formal off-site (one full day, no other agenda).
2. Re-read the north star.
3. Honestly diagnose what is wrong: market, channel, product, founder execution, or assumption.
4. Decide: continue with adjustments, or change strategy.
5. Document the decision. Tell anyone who needs to know (each other; later, customers; later, audience).

This is not "we fail and quit." It is "we refuse to drift past evidence of a problem."

## How we draw founder pay

For now: nothing. Both founders draw zero studio salary.

This is sustainable because of the FIRE financial position. It also keeps us honest: we cannot pretend Workbench is a viable business when revenue is just covering founder lifestyle. Until Workbench is genuinely paying for itself plus generating profit, founder draw stays at zero.

When does this change? When Workbench has 12 consecutive months of profit beyond all operating costs. At that point, we begin paying ourselves a small fixed salary from studio profits. Conservative ratchet up over time.

## How we treat windfalls

Profits beyond founder draws and operating costs go to:

1. **Cash buffer** — until the studio holds 12 months of total operating costs in its own account.
2. **Studio investments** — trade shows, equipment, contractors when justified.
3. **Founder profit distributions** — only after both 1 and 2 are healthy.

We do not reinvest aggressively into growth simply because we can. The portfolio thesis is built on patience and quality, not growth-at-all-costs.

## What we will not do

- Take on debt for the studio. Ever.
- Raise institutional capital, even at favorable terms. The runway exists; the discipline is to use it.
- Use FIRE savings as collateral for studio risk we are not willing to lose.
- Treat 10-year runway as a license to coast. The runway is fuel for patient correctness, not patient drift.
- Hide the financial truth from each other. Founders review this file together at each monthly retro.
