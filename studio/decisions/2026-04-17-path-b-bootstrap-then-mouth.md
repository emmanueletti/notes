# Path (b): in-person bootstrap, word-of-mouth scale

**Date:** 2026-04-17
**Reversibility:** Effectively irreversible for the next 12 months

## Context

In-person store visits are the only channel converting in our market. AI cannot multiply in-person sales. The studio's stated operating model is "small team + AI leverage" with a hard ceiling around 15 people.

This creates a real tension: how do we scale Workbench past the ceiling of two founders' weekly visit capacity (~50 visits/week) without breaking the operating model?

## Options considered

1. **Path (a):** Accept the operating-model exception permanently. Eventually hire a regional sales rep — one non-AI-leveraged human, treated as an exception. Workbench scales via humans walking into stores indefinitely.
2. **Path (b):** Treat in-person as the bootstrap channel only. Push hard on it for the first ~200 customers, then let word-of-mouth + trade reputation + portfolio brand carry the load. Operating model bends temporarily, then snaps back.
3. **Path (c):** Accept that Workbench tops out at ~200-500 customers (whatever two founders can sustain) and that's fine — high-LTV vertical SaaS doesn't need 10,000 customers to be a great business.

## Decision

**Path (b).**

In-person bootstraps. Once word-of-mouth and trade reputation are real, in-person ramps down to a maintenance level. Operating model holds again.

Path (c) is also acceptable as a fallback — if word-of-mouth never compounds enough, a 250-customer Workbench is still a viable, profitable product. We do not need to "blitz scale."

## Why

- Path (b) preserves the studio's stated operating model in the long run. The exception is bounded.
- The first 200 customers will produce the case studies, referrals, and trade reputation that make in-person unnecessary thereafter — if the product is genuinely good.
- The portfolio thesis depends on building brand that compounds across products. In-person work generates the *kinds* of artifacts (case studies, trade-press features) that feed brand.
- Path (a) commits us to permanently breaking our own ceiling. If we ever take that step it should be a fully conscious, separately-considered decision — not an accidental drift.
- Path (c) is a graceful fallback rather than a primary plan.

## What this means we accept

- For 12-18 months, founder time goes heavily into in-person work. This is not "AI-leveraged" work and we should not pretend it is.
- We must invest deliberately in word-of-mouth-enabling assets during this period: spectacular case studies, an ecstatic-customer focus, trade publications, podcast appearances, partner relationships.
- The transition from in-person primary to word-of-mouth primary is itself a milestone we should look for — not just "more customers," but "first inbound from referral," "first month with >25% inbound mix," etc.

## Revisit trigger

Revisit this decision if:

- At month 18, word-of-mouth still produces <10% of new customers and trade reputation has not materialized. → Honestly consider whether Path (a) or Path (c) is the actual path.
- A single hire becomes obviously the right move and would not violate the spirit of the studio. → Discuss explicitly, do not drift into it.
- The portfolio thesis itself proves wrong (no second product is justified, brand does not compound across products). → Reconsider whether Path (a) is fine after all.
