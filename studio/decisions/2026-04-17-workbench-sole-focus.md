# Workbench is sole product focus until profitable

**Date:** 2026-04-17
**Reversibility:** Reversible by explicit conscious decision; not by drift

## Context

The studio's portfolio thesis envisions multiple products over a decade. The founders have several product ideas in mind beyond Workbench, including Winkly (solo-tech booking software, currently captured in `~/notes/winkly.md`).

The temptation to start a second product before the first is self-running is the single most common failure mode for portfolio-style indie studios.

## Options considered

1. **Workbench only.** Full attention until Workbench is profitable + self-running. Then start product #2.
2. **Workbench primary, Winkly secondary.** Spend 80% on Workbench, 20% on Winkly.
3. **Concurrent products from day one.** Build the portfolio in parallel.

## Decision

**Option 1.** Workbench is the sole active product. Winkly and all other ideas are parked.

Definition of "self-running" for the purposes of unparking another product:

- Consistently profitable beyond all operating costs (12 consecutive months).
- Word-of-mouth or other compounding channel produces meaningful share of new customers (>25% inbound).
- Founder hours required to operate (not grow) Workbench are <20% of one founder's week.

When all three are true, product #2 may be considered.

## Why

- Stated explicitly in `~/notes/studio/operating-model.md`: "Do not start product #2 until product #1 is profitable and mostly running on rails. The temptation to start the next thing is the single biggest portfolio-company killer."
- Two founders splitting attention across products will under-serve both. The math is not "we can do half each" — it's "neither product gets the obsessive care that makes it loved."
- The portfolio brand is built by *one beloved product at a time*, not by a constellation of mediocre ones. A24 doesn't release 30 movies a year.
- Workbench is showing signal in a real market with real incumbents to displace. That is the obligation. Honor it.

## What this means we accept

- Product #2 ideas are captured (not lost) but not worked on. They go into a parked-ideas log.
- If a parked idea becomes urgent in our minds, that itself is a signal to examine — usually it means Workbench feels less interesting in the moment, which is the comfort-seeking response to hard work.
- Time spent thinking about parked products is time not spent on Workbench. Be honest about this.
- This decision is reversible only by an explicit, formal off-site conversation — not by gradual drift into "well maybe just a little prototype on the side."

## Revisit trigger

Revisit when **all three** of the unparking conditions above are true. Check at each quarterly direction-setting.

Also revisit (without unparking) if:

- Workbench is shown to be a non-viable bet (multiple missed milestones, channel breaking down). At that point the question becomes: is product #2 a better bet, or is the studio thesis itself wrong?
