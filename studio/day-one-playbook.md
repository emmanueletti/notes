# Day One Playbook

The concrete setup. Adapt to taste, but hold the shape.

---

## AI systems

**Build the tool, not the workflow.** Most people use AI ad-hoc and get ad-hoc results. Set up systems where AI has context.

- **Engineering:** Claude Code with a CLAUDE.md per project — codebase conventions, decisions, "don'ts". Pays back 10x within weeks.
- **Thinking partner:** A Claude project (or `~/notes/jarvis/`) per active product, loaded with the north star, current users, decisions log, open questions. Use it as the co-founder when the actual co-founder is asleep.
- **Knowledge base:** One source of truth (this notes directory). Everything decision-worthy goes here. AI is only as good as what we feed it.
- **Design:** v0, Lovable, or similar for fast UI iteration. Hand-tune the final 20% — that is where taste lives and where AI flattens us toward generic.
- **Voice/writing:** Do not outsource the public voice to AI. Use it to edit and pressure-test, never to draft. The written voice is half the brand.
- **Skip for now:** agents that "run the business," elaborate automations, anything we would need to maintain. Premature.

The principle: **AI is leverage on judgment we already have, not a substitute for judgment we do not.**

## Personal mindset

- **Think in years, work in weeks.** Decade-scale ambition, weekly-scale execution. Anything in between is anxiety.
- **Output is the only metric.** Not hours, not effort, not how hard it felt. Did something ship? Did a user love it more this week than last?
- **Taste is a daily practice.** We do not get taste from working — we get it from consuming great work, in and out of the domain. Schedule it like a meeting.
- **Refuse urgency that is not ours.** Most "urgent" is someone else's poor planning. Protect the long game.
- **Public by default.** Build in public. Ship in public. Write in public. Distribution is the hardest problem; start solving it day one, not at launch.
- **Small and shipped beats big and planned.** Always.

## Personal habits

- **First 3 hours: maker work.** No email, no Slack, no meetings. The most important thing, done before the world wakes up.
- **One user conversation a day, minimum.** Non-negotiable early on. We cannot build for users we do not talk to.
- **Ship something visible weekly.** A feature, a post, a teardown, a video. Cadence trains the muscle and builds audience.
- **Write 30 minutes daily.** Not for output — for clarity. We find our real opinions only by writing them.
- **Read 30 minutes daily, outside the domain.** Taste comes from cross-pollination. A24 founders watch foreign films, not just other A24 movies.
- **Walk every day.** Boring advice. Load-bearing.
- **Hard stop on work.** Pick a time. Honor it. Burnout is the only ending we cannot recover from quickly.

## Weekly rhythm

- **Mon AM:** Co-founder weekly. 60 min max. Review last week, set this week's one priority each, surface blockers. Async-first the rest of the week.
- **Mon-Thu:** Maker days. No meetings except user calls. Ship work.
- **Fri AM:** Ship-and-publish. Whatever shipped this week, talk about publicly. Even if small.
- **Fri PM:** Weekly review. What worked, what did not, what to change. 30 min solo, then 30 min together.
- **Weekend:** Off, mostly. One of us will sneak in work. Resist when we can.

Targets to hold ourselves to:

- 5+ user conversations/week
- 1+ public artifact/week (post, demo, teardown)
- 1+ shipped improvement/week to the live product
- 30-60 min daily outbound block

## Pace

- **6-week cycles, 2-week cooldown.** Shape Up model. Plan a cycle, build it, then cool down — clean up, explore, recover, plan the next. No infinite backlog grooming.
- **One product until it is self-sustaining.** Do not start product #2 until #1 is profitable and mostly running on rails. The temptation to start the next thing is the single biggest portfolio-company killer.
- **Sprints are emergencies.** If we are sprinting often, something is wrong upstream. Default cadence should be sustainable forever.
- **Quarterly direction setting.** One day, off-site (even if "off-site" is a coffee shop). Re-read the north star. Ask: are we still doing the thing? What would we cut if we were starting today?

## Operational systems

Set up day one, never think about again:

- **Money:** Separate business account. Bookkeeping software (Wave is free, fine for now). Accountant lined up before first revenue, not after. Runway tracker — months of cash, updated monthly. Pay ourselves a small fixed amount from day one, even if it is $500/mo. Establishes the principle.
- **Legal:** Incorporate properly. Operating agreement between the two of us covering equity, IP, what happens if one leaves. Do this before there is anything to fight about.
- **Decisions:** One markdown file per significant decision. Title, date, context, options considered, choice, why. Invaluable at month 18 when we cannot remember why we did something.
- **Users:** A way to talk to them from day zero. Email list, Discord, whatever. Own the channel — do not depend on a platform.
- **Tools:** Pick a stack we can iterate in fast. Boring is good. Resist the urge to choose tech because it is interesting.
- **Backups and security:** Password manager, 2FA on everything, automated backups. Boring, load-bearing.

## What to not do on day one

- Do not build internal tools. Use other people's.
- Do not optimize for scale we do not have.
- Do not worry about pricing yet — worry about whether anyone wants it.
- Do not build in stealth. Distribution starts now.
- Do not read more startup advice than we ship work.

## The meta-principle

The setup matters less than holding it. Pick a rhythm we can sustain when we are tired, sick, or discouraged — because we will be all three, often.

The companies that win are not the ones with the best day-one setup. They are the ones still running the same simple system in year three, while everyone else has burned out, pivoted, or quit.
