## The strategic shape

**Path (b) committed:** in-person store visits bootstrap the first ~200 customers. Word-of-mouth, trade reputation, and portfolio brand carry the load past that point.

This is a conscious exception to the studio's "small team + AI leverage" operating model. The market signal is clear — jewelry/watch shop owners buy from people they have met in person, not from cold digital outreach. We bend the operating model to fit the market truth, not the other way around.

Long-term, this still works because:

- The first 200 customers will produce the case studies, referrals, and trade reputation that make in-person unnecessary thereafter.
- The portfolio brand we build during this phase will benefit every future product.
- Once word-of-mouth is the engine, the team stays small and the operating model holds again.

## Pricing strategy

**Current state (2026-04-17):**
- $40 CAD/mo and $400 CAD/yr for the 10 existing customers.

**Test (effective immediately for new customers):**
- $60 CAD/mo
- $600 CAD/yr (saves 2 months at the new price)

**Why:** RepairDesk starts at $99 USD. The Edge is $4,600 upfront. Workbench at $40 CAD (~$29 USD) is materially underpriced for the value delivered. Acquisition cost via in-person visits is high (3-5 founder hours per customer); LTV must reflect this.

**Watch:** conversion rate at the new price. If conversion holds, raise again to $80 in 60 days. If conversion drops sharply, hold and investigate.

**Annual incentive:** the goal is a strong annual mix. Each annual customer reduces churn risk and improves cash position.

## Geographic strategy

**Phase 1 (now):** Toronto first. Highest density of jewelry/watch shops in Canada, both founders accessible.

**Phase 2:** Montreal. Bilingual EN/FR is a real edge there. Most other software competitors do not have French support.

**Phase 3:** Vancouver, Calgary, Ottawa. Lower density but still meaningful.

**Phase 4:** US cities. NYC diamond district (47th Street area), LA jewelry district, Chicago. Trade show concentration also drives where US customers come from.

## Trade show strategy

We will attend trade shows. Specifically:

- **Major bet:** JCK Las Vegas (May, annual). The largest jewelry trade show in North America. Booth presence cheap-but-credible. Goal: 50+ direct conversations, 5-10 trial signups, brand exposure with industry press.
- **Regional bets:** Centurion (jeweler-focused), AGS Conclave, CJA (Canadian Jewellers Association) events.

Plan a trade show 6 months out. Booth design, demo polish, case study materials all need to be ready in advance.

Budget for the first major trade show: $5-15K all-in. This is acceptable given the runway and the channel reality.

## Public voice strategy

The portfolio brand thesis says we need a public voice that compounds. For Workbench specifically, the audience is **the trade**, not Twitter or Hacker News. So the channels are:

- **Trade publications.** JCK Magazine, National Jeweler, Professional Jeweler, Canadian Jeweller. Pitched guest posts about specific shop-owner problems we have learned about.
- **Bench-jeweler podcasts.** Founder-as-guest. Share what we have learned from talking to shops, not pitch the product.
- **In-person speaking.** Eventually — talks at trade events. After we have a real case study and credibility.
- **A founder-signed newsletter or blog** about the trade. Slow build but the asset that compounds.

We do **not** invest meaningful time in:

- Twitter / general social media (wrong audience for Workbench)
- Hacker News / Indie Hackers (wrong audience for Workbench, though useful for studio brand later)
- Generic content marketing / SEO (slow, generic, low-leverage at our stage)

This may seem counter to the broader studio "build in public" principle. The reconciliation: Workbench's audience does not live online; the studio's audience eventually will. We invest in trade-press for Workbench's customers, and we will build studio-brand voice in adjacent channels (founder Twitter, indie founder community) for the longer-term portfolio play.

## Partnership strategy

People who already walk into jewelry shops:

- **Diamond dealers / wholesalers.** Visit shops to sell stones. Could refer Workbench. Find 3 in Toronto first.
- **Casting houses / model makers.** Shops send work to them. Mutual customer base.
- **Equipment suppliers** (laser welders, ultrasonic cleaners, polishing tools). Same shops are buyers.
- **Trade associations themselves.** Member benefit positioning.

Plant conversations now. Harvest in 6-12 months. Do not expect immediate referrals — these are slow trust-building relationships.

## What we will not do

- Hire a salesperson before reaching the milestones honestly with founders doing the work.
- Run paid ads. Wrong fit for the buyer.
- Build integrations before customers are paying for them.
- Add features hoping to unlock growth (almost never the bottleneck).
- Expand to electronics or automotive repair shops. The vertical is the wedge.
- Pitch big-chain retailers. Operating model wrong, sales cycle wrong.
- Compromise the in-person depth to chase volume.

## What success looks like at Day 90

- 25-35 paying customers (from current 10)
- 100+ in-person visits completed
- Documented playbook (opening, demo, objections, conversion conversation)
- One case study published with hard numbers
- Pricing test live with measurable data
- One trade show booked
- 12+ public artifacts shipped (one per week)
- Founders not burnt out

If 5+ of these are missed: full-day off-site, no other agenda, diagnose honestly.

## What success looks like at Day 90 — meta

The deeper success measure: do we trust the in-person channel more or less than we did at day 1? Do we know our customers better? Have we earned the right to keep going for another 90 days?

If yes — Phase 2.
If no — name what is wrong before continuing.
