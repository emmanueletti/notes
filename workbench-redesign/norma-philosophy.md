# Norma: what they believe

Read 2026-11-12 from normadesign.it/en: home, Log (Nothing special, Black and white, Every Internet page emits CO2, Websites must be accessible, Farewell to design, Ideas for an anti-browser, The present in a kaleidoscope), Legacy, and the service pages (graphic design, web, branding, books).

## Core stance

- "Removing aesthetic choice from the transmission of information." The studio "does absolutely nothing special."
- Nothing special: design has become a race to make things unexpected, and when everyone wants to be unique everyone ends up the same. Their model is Jasper Morrison and Naoto Fukasawa's "Super Normal": a table with four legs, one at each corner, nothing unexpected.
- Accept the most obvious choice. Reuse solutions that are proven to work, over and over.
- Lineage: Default Systems (Giampietro 2003), normcore (K-HOLE 2013), Fronzoni, Munari, Mari, Otl Aicher, Helmut Schmid, Vignelli.
- They separate stance from style. Grids, sobriety and limited palettes copied from the "good young Swiss" without the ideology are just another trend ("neo-Swiss extravagance").

## Type

- Typography is "the most precise medium to convey information."
- The literal manifesto: Arial and Times New Roman, single weight, black and white, blue underlined links, two lines of CSS, no framework.
- They mock chasing new fonts: "abcdinamo sans-serifs", the flood of Grotesks for an ephemeral market, "a font an ECAL student just created."
- The public can't tell one Helvetica version from another.

## Colour

- Design in black and white or greyscale; it forces precise decisions.
- Colour carries meanings you can't control (is green nature or money?).
- The only colour that survives is colour as a signal: a yellow helmet, a blue link.
- "Corporate colour" is a branding myth, like the need for a logo and an icon set.

## Images and branding

- Photos and images only when necessary, to support the text. Their own images are dithered to save weight.
- Brand identity mockups (letterheads, stationery flat-lays) are fake and were never useful to anyone.
- A logo as symbolism is useless. The best way to show quality is to sell a quality product; the name can be written roughly in pencil.

## The web

- Speed and weight are respect. Their site loads in 43ms and weighs 7KB, with no cookies and no cookie banner.
- Accessibility (WCAG) is a baseline. They admit their own past work failed it.
- They list what disrespects users: pop-ups, cookie walls, sticky headers taking 80-100px, carousels, autoplay, tracking, prechecked newsletter boxes, share prompts, "download our app" walls.
- They reject the template site: full-page photo with a centred title, dropdown menu, three service columns each with an icon, stock photos.
- Static HTML where nothing is interactive (they rebuilt on Eleventy).

## Nuance

- It isn't pure anti-design. The service pages say the goal is work at the level of London or Zurich, and that how text is set communicates before it's read; amateur-looking work loses.
- "Communicating in an honest way is much more important than the colour you choose."
