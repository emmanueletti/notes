# Workbench: discovery outreach templates

Built 2026-09-21. Companion to `workbench/docs/rethinking-workbench.md`. Sister doc to
`belly-pro-outreach-templates.md`, which these are adapted from.

Goal: learn what independent jewellery businesses actually need, from people who are not family.

## 1. This is a different interview from Belly's, in one important way

Belly Pro does not exist, so those calls are greenfield discovery. Nothing to defend, no ego on the
table, and the worst outcome is a wasted twenty minutes.

Workbench exists, is in daily use, and has been sold. That changes the failure mode. The risk is no
longer asking the wrong questions. The risk is talking. A founder with a built product in front of
a prospect will start explaining it inside four minutes, and the moment that happens the call stops
producing information and starts producing politeness.

Rule for every call below: the product does not come up until the prospect has finished describing
their own work, and ideally not at all. If they ask what it does, answer in one sentence and return
the question.

## 2. Before writing anything, decide what is actually broken

The premise going in is that Workbench is mispositioned and is not what independents want. That may
be right. It is worth noticing that `rethinking-workbench.md` from 2026-07-29 reaches a different
conclusion: every customer opens it daily, daily active B2B usage is the strongest retention signal
there is, and distribution is the bottleneck rather than the product. The same doc also warns that
the founders were grading a working product as a failure, and that the perception gap was doing
more damage than the market was.

Both readings can be partly true. Nine daily users is real, and most of them being family means the
sample cannot tell us whether strangers want this. Two strangers is not enough to settle it either.

So do not run these calls to confirm that the product is wrong. Run them to separate three
hypotheses, which need completely different responses:

H1, positioning. The product is close to right and the words are wrong. The site says "repair
shops" and pulls in legacy retail, which haggles and then asks for The Edge's feature list.
Signal to listen for: you describe the product plainly, in their language, and the reaction changes
sharply from what the website produced. "Wait, it does that?" is the tell.

H2, product. The feature set is aimed at a workflow the real ICP does not have. Signal: you
describe it plainly and they still shrug, or they walk you through a workflow with a stage in it
that Workbench has no concept of.

H3, distribution. Right product, right words, nobody has heard of it because two people have never
industrialized either channel that worked. Signal: they want it, they would pay, and they simply do
not look for software and never encountered us.

Every template below ends with what to watch for. Tally the three at the end of the week. The answer
decides whether the next month is a rewrite, a rewrite of the site, or a sales motion.

## 3. The pools, in order of signal quality

### Past rejections, highest signal, go first

`rethinking-workbench.md` records that most outreach ended in rejection. Those people are the most
valuable interview pool available and the one founders avoid, because the calls are uncomfortable.

They already know who we are, so the opener needs no explaining. They have already made a decision,
so their reasoning is concrete rather than hypothetical. And a "why did you say no" email from a
founder who is visibly not trying to re-sell has a high reply rate, because it is unusual and it is
flattering to be asked.

Go through the email archive and list every shop that went quiet or said no. That list is the
week's primary target.

### Sarah Bijoux specifically

Sarah gave the single most useful objection we have on record, that going through a system to send
renderings and estimates makes the experience impersonal, and then named the real pain when asked,
which was the time it takes to file things in Drive.

That conversation is not finished. She is the closest thing we have to the ICP, she is local, and
she has already proven she will say the true thing. One more sitting with her, specifically about
the custom workflow end to end, is worth more than five cold calls.

### Existing non-family customers

Jewellery Clinic, Russell Jewellers, Florence at Sarah Bijoux. Three people who chose this with
their own money and no relationship obligation.

Ask them two things nobody asks customers: what almost stopped you, and what did you assume it did
before you started using it. The second question is the cleanest positioning test that exists,
because the gap between what a buyer expected and what they got is exactly the positioning error.

### Family customers

Use for workflow detail only. They will describe an intake or a bench day accurately and for free.

Never use them for willingness to pay, for positioning reactions, or as evidence that the product is
wanted. `rethinking-workbench.md` is explicit that family customers use it because we built it.
Anything they say about price or about whether they would have bought it carries no information.

### Cold independent jewellery brands

The ICP from section 4 of the strategy doc. Founder-operated, one to eight people, atelier rather
than retail counter, Shopify native, Instagram-led, does repairs plus a growing bespoke practice.

Screen out the two adjacent segments deliberately. Legacy storefront jewellers are a different buyer
with different behaviour, and hobbyist or side-hustle makers have no budget. Underdog does not mean
broke.

## 4. Channels, weighted by what has already worked

### In person, the channel that already converted and was never industrialized

The best customer came from an in-person conversation. That is the single most actionable line in
the strategy doc and it has not been acted on.

Jewellers work at a bench with their hands occupied and are hard to reach by email for exactly that
reason. They are easy to reach by walking in during a quiet hour. Montreal is the home market and
Sarah Bijoux is on Saint-Laurent, so the studio-dense strips are walkable: Saint-Laurent, Mile End,
Old Montreal, and the Plateau. Toronto has the same shape around the design district and Dundas
West. Vancouver around Main and Gastown.

Go on a weekday afternoon, not a Saturday. Ask for ten minutes, not a meeting. Bring nothing to
show, because bringing a laptop turns it into a demo.

### The workflow shadow, the highest-yield ask in this whole doc

For a trade this physical, watching beats asking. Thirty minutes standing in a studio while someone
takes in a repair or opens a custom file tells you more than an hour of an interview, because people
cannot accurately describe a process they do on autopilot.

It also sounds like a smaller ask than it is, and jewellers tend to say yes because being watched
while doing skilled work is flattering rather than intrusive.

Template E below is for this.

### Instagram

The ICP is Instagram-led by definition. A brand operator under 45 reads DMs and ignores email.

Search by tag and city: torontojeweller, montrealjewelry, bijouxmontreal, vancouverjeweller,
customengagementring plus the city, goldsmith plus the city, and the local hashtags studios use for
market and pop-up announcements.

Screen the same way as in `belly-pro-outreach-list.md`. A studio with a Shopify link in bio, custom
or bespoke consultations bookable, and a visible bench is the target. A page selling only
ready-to-ship under $80 with no custom work is not.

### Trade and community bodies

- Canadian Jewellers Association, the national trade body since 1918, over 1,000 member locations,
  with a searchable member directory: https://canadianjewellers.com/
- Metal Arts Guild, Toronto, founded 1946, runs the Medium is Metal exhibition series. Contemporary
  and studio-maker leaning rather than retail, which matches the ICP better than CJA does.
- Vancouver Metal Arts Association, a community of metalsmiths and jewellers:
  https://www.vancouvermetalarts.com/

Note the skew. CJA is retail and wholesale heavy and will surface the legacy segment we decided not
to chase. The guild and association routes surface studio makers, which is closer to the ICP but
tips toward the hobbyist end. Neither is clean, so use both and screen hard on whether the studio
actually runs repairs and bespoke as a business.

### Schools, for the pipeline rather than for this week

Graduates start studios, and a studio in year two is a buyer with no incumbent system and no habits
to unlearn.

- George Brown, Toronto. One of the largest jewellery schools in North America, 170 full-time
  students: https://www.georgebrown.ca/business-creative-industries-culinary-arts/design/jewellery
- École de joaillerie de Montréal, with Cégep du Vieux Montréal since 1989:
  https://ecoledejoaillerie.com/
- Vancouver Metal Art School.

Not a source of interviews this week. Worth a relationship later, and worth remembering that
alumni networks are how this trade actually talks.

### Shopify

The ICP is Shopify native. That is a research shortcut rather than a channel: a jewellery site
running Shopify with a bespoke consultation booking flow is a pre-qualified prospect, and you can
tell from the page source in ten seconds.

## 5. Research step, two minutes per prospect

1. Confirm they do repairs, bespoke, or both. If the site sells only finished stock, skip them.
2. Confirm size. One to eight people. A team page or an About with a single founder photo is the
   tell. A chain with locations is out.
3. Check whether the storefront runs Shopify.
4. Note one specific thing: a piece, a collection, a workshop they run, the fact that consultations
   are by appointment. That is the opening line.
5. Check whether they are livelier on Instagram than on email, and send to the livelier one.

## 6. Email templates

### A. Past rejection or gone quiet, run these first

Subject: why you passed on Workbench

Hi [Name],

We spoke [rough timeframe] about Workbench and it did not go anywhere. I am not writing to try
again.

We are rethinking who the product is for and I would rather learn from the people who said no than
from the handful who said yes. Twenty minutes, and the only thing I want is the honest version of
why it was not right.

There is nothing to buy and I will not pitch you. If it turns out we should have built something
different, that is the answer I am looking for.

[Your name]
[Studio name], [city]

Reply with no thanks and I will not write again.

What to watch for. If the reason was price or features, that is H2. If the reason was "I thought it
was for repair shops and I am not a repair shop", that is H1 and it is the most likely answer.

### B. Existing non-family customer

Subject: two questions I should have asked you earlier

Hi [Name],

You have been using Workbench for [length of time] and I have never asked you the two questions that
would actually help us.

What almost stopped you from starting? And what did you assume it did before you used it?

Happy to do it in twenty minutes on a call, or in writing if that is easier. This is not a check-in
about your account and there is nothing to renew.

[Your name]
[Studio name], [city]

What to watch for. The gap between what they assumed and what they got is the positioning error,
stated directly by a buyer. Write it down verbatim.

### C. Cold independent jewellery brand

Subject: how you keep track of a custom job

Hi [Name],

I came across [studio name] through [specific: Instagram, the CJA directory, walking past your
studio on Saint-Laurent] and saw that you take on bespoke work alongside [collection or repairs].

I am one of two people behind Workbench, a tool for jewellery studios. We are rethinking who it is
for, and before we change anything I want to understand how a custom job actually moves through a
studio like yours. Where the sketches live, where the client emails live, how you know what stage a
piece is at.

Could I ask you about it for twenty minutes? There is nothing to buy and I will not demo anything.
In return I will send you a summary of what comes out of all the conversations. Nothing you tell me
is attributed to you or shared with another studio.

[Your name]
[Studio name], [city]

Reply with no thanks and I will not write again.

What to watch for. Count the systems they name. If it is a binder, a Drive folder, a group chat and
a spreadsheet, the incumbent is exactly what the strategy doc says it is, which supports H1 and H3
over H2.

### D. Sarah Bijoux, the follow-up that is owed

Not a template. Ask for a sitting, in person, at the studio, and go through one complete custom
project start to finish with the actual files open. She already told us the client-facing surface is
the problem and that filing speed is the pain. What we do not have is the shape of the mess behind
it, in enough detail to build for it.

Go with no laptop and no product talk. Ask her to open the real Drive folder and the real binder.

### E. Workflow shadow request, use on anyone who has already replied warmly

Hi [Name],

Rather than take twenty minutes of your time talking, would it be more useful if I came and watched?

Half an hour at your bench while you take in a repair or open a custom file. I ask nothing, I just
want to see how it actually works rather than how people describe it. I can come at whatever hour is
quiet for you.

[Your name]

What to watch for. Everything they do that they did not mention in the interview. That gap is the
product.

## 7. Instagram DM templates

No link in the first message. Confidentiality promise goes in the second, once they reply, as in the
Belly doc.

### G. Cold brand operator

Hi [Name], I saw [specific piece or that you do bespoke consultations]. I am one of two people
behind Workbench, a tool for jewellery studios, and we are rethinking who it is for. Before we
change anything I am asking studios how a custom job actually moves through the shop, where the
sketches and client emails end up. Could I ask you about it? Nothing to buy and no demo.

### H. In person, said out loud

Use this walking into a studio. It is shorter than it looks written down.

Hi, I am [Name], I am local and I build software for jewellery studios. I am not selling anything
today. I am trying to understand how a custom job actually moves through a shop like this. Do you
have ten minutes, or should I come back at a quieter hour?

## 8. The question set

This is a repositioning interview, so reconstruct history rather than collect opinions. What
someone did last month is evidence. What they say they would like is not.

Open with the last real job, not with a general question.

1. Walk me through the last custom piece you finished. Start from the first message the client sent.
2. Where did the sketches go? Where did the renderings go? Where did the client's emails go?
3. At any point that week, if I had asked you what stage that piece was at, where would you have
   looked?
4. Now the last repair. Same thing, from the moment it came over the counter.
5. What is the last job you lost track of, or the last time a client called to ask where something
   was?
6. What did you try before the current setup, and why did you stop using it?
7. If you disappeared for a week, could someone else in the studio pick up a job in progress?

Then, and only then, the two that test positioning:

8. If I described a tool as [one plain sentence, in their words, no product name], would that be
   something you would look at?
9. What would you have to see for it to be worth changing what you do now?

Question 6 is the one that separates H2 from H3. A studio that has tried three tools and abandoned
them has a product objection. A studio that has never looked for software has a distribution
problem, and no amount of rebuilding fixes it.

## 9. What not to do

Do not demo. Not on the call, not on the walk-in, not when they ask nicely. One sentence and back
to their workflow.

Do not bring a laptop into a studio. It converts a conversation into a sales call the moment it
opens.

Do not defend the product when someone criticises it. The instinct will be strong because the
criticism will sometimes be wrong or out of date. Write it down and say thank you. A corrected
prospect stops talking.

Do not ask what features they want. They will answer, the answer will be a worse version of what
they already use, and it will feel like progress.

Do not count family answers as data, in either direction.

Do not lead with "repair shops" in any message. That is the exact phrasing the strategy doc
identifies as dragging in the wrong segment, and it would poison the sample in the same way it
poisoned the funnel.

Do not walk into a retail counter with a display case out front and no bench. Wrong segment,
politely.

## 10. What to bring back

Per call, one page:

- The list of systems they named, counted
- The last thing that went wrong, verbatim
- What they tried and abandoned
- Their reaction to the one plain sentence, verbatim
- Which of H1, H2 or H3 the call supports

At ten calls, the tally decides the next month. That is the deliverable, not a feature list.

## 11. CASL

Same as the Belly doc. Canadian anti-spam law exempts a message sent to a person engaged in a
commercial activity that consists solely of an inquiry relating to that activity, which is what
every template here is, provided it stays an inquiry and does not become a pitch.

Template A is worth a second look on this point, because it goes to people we previously tried to
sell to. Keep it clean of any offer, any price and any product claim, and it stays an inquiry. Every
template carries sender identification and a plain opt-out line regardless. Keep both in.
