# Belly: 30-day growth plan

Written 2026-09-30. Covers 2026-10-01 to 2026-10-30.

## 1. Context and goal

Over the next 30 days we run four growth experiments in parallel, measure each
one, and pick a winner to double down on. The goal is to go from 150 to 300-400
users and, more importantly, to know which channel brings users who stay.

Where we are:

- 150 users, all from the cofounder's personal social posts.
- Videos are getting 200-300 views. That is normal for a small account. It
  means the platform shows each video to a test audience and does not push it
  further, which is usually a hook problem in the first 2 seconds rather than a
  topic problem.
- We have not tested any channel besides founder content.

The four experiments:

1. Content, run as a test instead of just posting
2. A paid micro-creator test
3. One product growth loop
4. Communities and coach conversations

Who we are talking to: people whose recipes are scattered across screenshots,
TikTok saves, Instagram bookmarks, blogs and notes apps, and who want to plan
the week and shop once. Busy professionals, couples sharing meals, meal
preppers, budget cooks. The pain is "I save recipes and never cook them" and
"what's for dinner?" at 6pm.

## 2. Week 1: measurement and user interviews

Without attribution, the day-30 review is guesswork, so this comes first.

- Add "How did you hear about Belly?" to signup as a dropdown: TikTok,
  Instagram, Pinterest, YouTube, a creator, Reddit, a friend shared something,
  a coach, other.
- Give every channel and every creator its own tracked link or code.
- Pull week-1 and week-4 retention for the current 150 users. If recent users
  drop off fast, fixing onboarding comes before any acquisition spend.
- Talk to the 10 most active users, by call or DM. Ask why they keep using
  Belly, how they would describe it to a friend, and what they used before.

Write their exact words down. They become video hooks, app store copy and
creator briefs.

## 3. Content: run it as a test

Keep posting, but treat each video as an experiment with a measured result.

Cadence: 1 short video a day. Film 7-10 in one batch session each week so it
does not eat every day.

Diagnose first: check 3-second hold and average watch time in analytics. If
most viewers leave in the first 2 seconds, rewrite the opening, not the topic.
Open on the result or the problem ("I had 400 saved recipes and cooked none of
them"), never on an introduction.

Test three formats, about 10 days each, and keep the winner:

1. Magic moment demo. Screenshot a recipe, Belly imports it, and the grocery
   list appears. Ten seconds that show the product doing something surprising.
2. A series. Series bring people back and build followers faster than one-off
   posts. Ideas:
   - "Clearing out my 400 saved recipes, day 1 of 30": import a few, cook one
   - "Planning a week of dinners in 10 minutes"
   - "Meal prep on $60 a week"
3. Reply and react. Reply to comments with a video, or stitch popular recipe
   videos by saving them into Belly. This borrows other accounts' reach.

Distribution: post every video to TikTok, Reels, Shorts and Pinterest.
Pinterest matters most for Belly. Recipes and meal planning are its core
content, and a pin keeps getting found for months instead of 48 hours.

Expectations: about 1 in 15 videos takes off. Judge the 30-day trend, not
single videos.

## 4. Creator test (weeks 2-4)

A small paid test to learn whether creators bring retained users at a cost we
can live with. Budget: $1,000-2,000.

Who:

- Recipe and food creators with 5k-50k followers. Their followers already save
  their recipes, and Belly is where those saves can go.
- Meal prep, budget cooking and "what I eat in a week" creators.

How:

1. Shortlist 30 creators.
2. DM all 30, aiming for 5-8 deals.
3. Offer $100-300 per post, or a free Patron membership plus a cut of any
   revenue they bring in.
4. Give each creator a tracked link or code.
5. The ask: share your recipes as a Belly playlist, not just a mention of the
   app. For a food creator that is useful to their followers, not a plain ad.

Measure signups and 30-day retention per creator, not views.

## 5. Growth loop (weeks 1-2, engineering)

Build one loop so that every share brings in new people.

- Public web page for a shared playlist or week plan, viewable without an
  account, with a "Save to Belly" button. Creators, coaches and users all feed
  it.
- After a user plans their first week, prompt them to share it with a partner
  or friend.
- Make inviting a partner to the grocery list a default action, not a buried
  one. Households are a natural second user.

Tag every share link with its source so signups from it show up in the
scoreboard.

## 6. Communities and coaches (weeks 3-4)

Low effort, run alongside everything else.

- Reddit: r/MealPrepSunday, r/EatCheapAndHealthy, r/Cooking, r/budgetfood.
  Post useful content and the founder story, not ads.
- Meal planning and meal prep groups on Facebook.
- 10 conversations with nutrition coaches and personal trainers who build meal
  plans for clients, as in `belly-for-professionals.md`. These are learning
  calls, not a launch. Coaches are the one channel where a single sale brings
  15-40 users.

Track weekly:

| Metric                    | Broken down by          |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Signups                   | Source                  |
| Week-1 retention          | Source                  |
| 3-second hold, avg watch  | Content format          |
| Saves and shares          | Content format          |
| Signups per creator       | Creator                 |
| Playlist page visits      | Share source            |

Day-30 review: rank channels by retained users per hour or dollar spent. Put
most of the next 60 days into the winner, keep the runner-up going, and drop
the rest.
